# Characters

The campaign is a cast, not a spreadsheet with names glued on. Agents are heuristics;
**characterization** is how we talk about them so a `100d` report feels like a port
cooperative memoir, not a unit test dump.

Inspiration strands:

- Owner-master memoir — [The Margin Was The Freedom](https://frankhaugen.github.io/galactic-confederation-review/articles/the-margin-was-the-freedom/)
- Registry / ship law — [Ship Law and Registry](https://frankhaugen.github.io/galactic-confederation-review/dossiers/ship-law-and-registry/)
- Full commercial weather — **The Calypso Cycle** refs ([calypso-canon.md](calypso-canon.md))

|-------|---------------|-------------|-----------------|

| **MV Bulk River** | Mega-hauler, fleet (not owner-master) | Weather with lawyers: punctual when it wants | Will not break schedule for four pallets and a frightened technician |

Household cohorts are not named captains. They are **the reason Final exists** — budgets,
wages, empty shelves as moral weather.

## Hulls as characters

### Light commercial / ST tramp

Cycle class: **Support Transport (ST)** — flexible, reliable, fast enough; not luxurious,
not fragile, not specialized. Owner-operator eligible. Sins: ~36 cargo `VehicleClass`.

They live in Varr’s middle space *and* James’s inheritance weather: open routes, real
constraints, three invoices from finished, debt that can follow the hull.

**Voice:** short sentences, dock time, distrust of romance, gratitude for registry
without loving insurers.

### Mega-hauler (`MV Bulk River`)

~120 cargo, SlowEconomic only. Corporate logistics archetype — tonne-plans, loops.
When Bulk River is idle, the Priority economy stands on a missing foundation.

**Voice:** schedule language, “loop added / loop dropped.”

## Archetypes (playable readings)

### The Owner-Master / Owner-Operator

All three invoices arrive addressed to the same person. Simple inside chain.
Outside world still has traffic rules, drive margins, renewals, **insurable range**.
**Tell:** one hull idle → one human-shaped failure on the registry table.

### The Underwriter (Meridian-shaped)

Not cruel. Actuarial. Drama-hating. Prices instability before it becomes obligation.
Praise: *Odd vessel. Stable risk.*
**Tell:** premiums rise with Priority + wear; claims pay without undoing loss.

### The Liaison (Ixa-shaped)

Reads underwriter language; makes the ship boringly compliant; letterhead ≠ power.
**Tell:** future work from “known responsive” after ugly standby completes.

### The Fixer / Continuity lead (Torrik / Varek-shaped)

Buys options while cheap. Soft pickup. Pattern break. Never theatrical.
**Tell:** drama milestones that spend money early so Finance complains quietly.

### The Dock / CCA clerk

Does not have to like you. Needs the record. Job boards behind glass — locked until
registration. Escrow math recited like weather.
**Tell:** `CanOperate` false is a closed door.

### The Big Carrier

Makes main routes livable. Leaves edges to tramps.
**Tell:** Slow biography while tramps burn Priority.

### The Small Port

“Can she carry it?” / “Ask if her cold hold works.” / “Do we still owe her?”
**Tell:** Sol export hub, soft/hard Raw caps.

| Acting choices | Agent `LastDecision` one-liners |
| Plot beats | `MILESTONE:` lines + mega biography |
| Underwriter | Station ledger via `InsurancePulse` |

Ventures (household → new tramp) = cast expansion: a new owner-master enters the
registry or fails trying — Willie as cautionary ghost.

## Writing rules

1. **No romance that kills ships.** Romantics buy hulls and skip inspections.
2. **Freedom ≠ comfort.** Allow both sentences in the same paragraph.
3. **Name the constraint.** Fuel, dock, premium, wear, due-now, escrow — not “bad luck.”
4. **Keep Ops/Core sacred.** Narrator never invents a single “total cash.”
5. **Prefer dock English over policy English** — unless you are Meridian, then prefer
   recorded euphemism (*protected passenger*, *selection support*, *degraded handoff*).
6. **Boring on purpose** is a feature. Escrow and registry are the stage, not the enemy.
