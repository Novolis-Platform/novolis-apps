# Space Fleet: Survey Team

**Space Fleet: Survey Team** is a mobile-native science-fiction survey game in which the player uses their phone as a live field instrument to map and certify real geographic regions.

The world is divided into broad terrain categories such as urban, suburban, forest, mountain, coast, and agricultural land. These categories determine the survey method. Urban regions use public streets and plazas; forests use trails and perimeter observations; mountains are surveyed remotely from safe viewpoints and lower elevations. The game never requires complete physical coverage or exact-coordinate collection.

During a survey, the player deliberately activates individual sensors:

* **Sonic sensor:** live waveform and frequency-band analysis from the microphone, with no retained audio.
* **Photonic sensor:** live luminance, colour, contrast, and spectral-style analysis from the camera, with no retained imagery.
* **Magnetic sensor:** field strength, direction, variation, and gradient measurements.
* **Spatial sensor:** GPS, motion, orientation, elevation, and route geometry.

The player gathers representative samples, walks safe transects, compares locations, and investigates broad anomaly zones. Progress depends on data quality, geographic diversity, and resolving uncertainties, not raw distance or touching every part of the map.

Each region follows a bounded loop:

**Survey → Detect → Resolve → Certify**

Once the required evidence is sufficient, the player classifies the terrain and submits the survey. Certification permanently marks the region as complete, while later visits may improve confidence or resolve optional findings.

The design deliberately avoids Pokémon Go-style incentives. It uses public-access corridors, tolerant sampling zones, remote observation, inferred terrain, and player-reported inaccessible areas. Private property and dangerous terrain are never required for completion.

The central reward is the transformation of an uncertain grey region into a completed piece of alien cartography.

> **Use real-world field data to make one more part of an unknown planet known.**
